# Budgeting guidance

The 50/30/20 rule suggests spending about 50% of take-home income on needs such as rent, groceries, bills and transport, 30% on wants such as dining out, shopping and entertainment, and saving or investing the remaining 20%.

Food delivery apps add up quickly because of delivery fees, platform charges and small impulse orders. Cooking at home for even three extra meals a week, or setting a fixed monthly cap for delivery apps, usually cuts food spending by 20 to 30 percent.

Subscriptions such as OTT platforms, music and cloud storage often go unused. Review them every quarter, cancel duplicates, and consider annual plans or family sharing for the ones you really use.

Build an emergency fund equal to three to six months of essential expenses before taking investment risk. Keep it in a savings account or liquid fund so it is available quickly.

Pay yourself first: set up an automatic SIP or recurring deposit on the day your salary arrives, so saving happens before spending. Even a small SIP started early benefits strongly from compounding.

For online shopping, use a 48-hour rule for non-essential purchases. If you still want the item after two days, buy it; this removes most impulse buys. Avoid saving cards on shopping apps.

Track small UPI payments. Many small daily payments for tea, snacks and rides look harmless but can make up 10 to 15 percent of monthly spending. Set a weekly UPI spending limit and review it every Sunday.

Reduce transport costs by using metro or shared rides for regular commutes, combining errands in one trip, and comparing cab and auto prices across apps before booking.

Groceries are cheaper when you plan a weekly list, buy staples in bulk, and compare quick-commerce prices with local stores. Quick-commerce convenience fees and small baskets raise the cost per item.

Utility and bill costs can be lowered by switching to a better mobile or broadband plan, using energy-efficient appliances, and paying bills on time to avoid late fees.

If spending in a category jumps sharply compared with the previous month, check for one-off events such as travel, festivals or gifts before treating it as a new habit. Recurring increases deserve a budget change.
